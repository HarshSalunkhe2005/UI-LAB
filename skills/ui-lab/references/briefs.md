# Briefs: how to ask, and how to write them

A brief is a few lines. Writing it is most of the work, because every later decision needs something to be measured against. The template is `templates/brief.md`; this file helps when the request is thin.

## The lines that matter most

1. **Who, where, mood.** "Commuters on a phone, mid-journey, impatient." Not "users". Mood in concrete words, not "modern, clean, professional".
2. **Mode.** Persuade (decide and act), Operate (finish a task), Read (understand something) or Experience (be inside the work). Pick one; it decides how much expression the surface can afford.
3. **The one memorable moment.** One sentence. "A board that changes while you watch."
4. **Real content.** The words, numbers and images that will be there. If they do not exist yet, write honest samples and list them in the handoff (never on the page).
5. **Constraints.** Stack, devices, languages, accessibility level, deadline, things already decided, things forbidden.

## One sharp question

If a line cannot be written, ask exactly one question that unblocks it. Good: "Is this for people deciding whether to buy, or people already using it?" Bad: "What do you want?" If the user is not available, state your assumption in the brief and carry on; say so in the handoff.

## Examples

**Night-train company.** Travellers on a laptop planning a trip, calm but excited. Persuade. A departures board that updates as you hover routes. Eleven real routes and prices. World 9 (Machine and Signal) against world 8 (Soft and Living).

**Finance newsletter.** Subscribers reading on the morning commute. Read. Reading is the interface: one essay, six market lines, three letters. Static and fast. World 12 (Paper and Print) against world 7 (Instrument and Spec).

**Hi-fi maker.** Enthusiasts at a desk with good headphones. Persuade. The deck plays, and you can switch its tape stage in and out. Specs, three finishes, a price. World 11 (Soft and Living) against world 3 (Machine and Signal).

## Smells that mean the brief is not ready

- "Make it premium" with no content. Ask for the words.
- Two worlds named at once as one direction. Choose one; use the other only as the second candidate at Gate 2.
- A hero that needs an asset nobody has. Name the cheapest honest substitute (drawn SVG, a live fragment of the product) and ask.
- "Like site X but ours." Learn the technique, never copy the layout or the content.
