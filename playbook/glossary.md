# Glossary: design and web words, in plain language

UI Lab uses a few terms that are common among designers and developers but unfamiliar elsewhere. Here they are, with no jargon.

**Pattern.** One reusable piece of interface: a button, a menu, a scroll effect, a whole page section. Every pattern in UI Lab runs live and shows its code.

**World.** A complete, consistent style for a site: its colours, typefaces, movement and what it refuses to do. "Editorial quiet luxury" and "departures board" are worlds. Choosing one is the single most important design decision.

**Signature move.** One striking technique that makes a site memorable, taken from a site we studied and rebuilt in our own code.

**Mode.** What a visitor is trying to do: *Persuade* (decide and act), *Operate* (finish a task), *Read* (understand something), *Experience* (be inside the work).

**Hero.** The first big section of a page, what you see before scrolling.

**Scroll-driven.** Something that changes as you scroll: a picture that turns, a line that draws, a heading that comes into focus.

**Parallax.** Layers that move at different speeds as you scroll, giving a sense of depth.

**Reduced motion.** A setting in your device that asks websites to tone down movement. Good sites honour it.

**Accessible.** Usable by everyone: people who use only a keyboard, a screen reader, a magnifier, or who have low vision, colour blindness or motion sensitivity.

**Screen reader.** Software that reads a page aloud and lets a person navigate it without seeing it.

**Focus (keyboard focus).** Which control is currently selected when you press Tab. It must always be visible.

**Contrast.** The difference in brightness between text and its background. Too little makes text hard to read.

**Token (design token).** A named design value such as a colour or a spacing step, defined once and reused everywhere, so a change in one place changes the whole site.

**WebGL / 3D.** The browser's way of drawing three-dimensional scenes on the graphics chip. Impressive, but heavy and harder to make accessible.

**Fontsource.** A way to include fonts in a project so they load from your own server rather than a third party.

**Teardown.** Taking a finished site apart to understand how it was built: its fonts, colours, libraries and the one idea it hangs on. UI Lab keeps fifty-plus of them.

**Vibe-coded.** A site or app generated mainly by prompting an AI tool. UI Lab studies the popular ones to see what works.

**Playbook.** The written procedure for designing well: the brief, inspiration, direction, build, and verification.

**Verify.** Actually running the thing and checking it: loading it fresh, scrolling every section, trying a phone width, using the keyboard, reading the console. Reading code is not verification.
