# Briefs: how to ask, and how to write the five lines

A brief is five lines. Writing it is most of the work, because every later decision needs something to be measured against.

## The five lines

1. **Who, where, mood.** "Commuters on a phone, mid-journey, impatient." Not "users".
2. **Mode.** Persuade, Operate, Read or Experience. Pick one; it decides how much expression the surface can afford.
3. **The one memorable moment.** One sentence. "A board that changes while you watch."
4. **Real content.** The words, numbers and images that will be there. If they do not exist yet, say so and write honest placeholders.
5. **Constraints.** Stack, devices, languages, deadline, things already decided, things forbidden.

## One sharp question

If a line cannot be written, ask exactly one question that unblocks it. Good: "Is this for people deciding whether to buy, or people already using it?" Bad: "What do you want?"

## Examples

**Night-train company.** Travellers on a laptop planning a trip, calm but excited. Persuade. A departures board that updates as you hover routes. Eleven real routes and prices. Next.js, no CMS. Route: world 9, proof `site-departures`.

**Finance newsletter.** Subscribers reading on the morning commute. Read. Reading is the interface. One essay, six market lines, three letters. Static and fast. Route: world 12, proof `site-ledger`.

**Hi-fi maker.** Enthusiasts at a desk with good headphones. Persuade. The deck plays, and you can switch its tape stage in and out. Specs, three finishes, a price. Route: world 11, proof `site-tape`.

## Smells that mean the brief is not ready

- "Make it premium" with no content. Ask for the words.
- Two worlds named at once. Choose one.
- A hero that needs an asset nobody has. Name the cheapest honest substitute and ask.
